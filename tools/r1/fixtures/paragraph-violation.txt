The investigation began on a Monday and almost immediately produced more questions than answers because the original file had been moved at least twice and possibly three times depending on which clerk you asked and which logbook you trusted, with one logbook contradicting another in ways that suggested either clerical sloppiness or deliberate misdirection, and the investigators were not yet ready to say which, although several of them privately believed it was the latter, since the pattern of contradictions seemed to favor a particular employee whose name kept appearing in the margins of the older logs, an employee who had left the company under unclear circumstances roughly eighteen months before the first complaint was filed, and whose forwarding address was a post office box in a town nobody on the investigative team had heard of, a town that turned out, on further inquiry, not to exist at all, which meant the address had been fabricated, which meant the departure had been planned, which meant the original complaint and the missing file were almost certainly related in ways that nobody had yet been willing to articulate in writing, and so the investigation continued, expanding outward day by day, drawing in more interviews and more cross-references and more redacted documents, until the room reserved for the case files no longer held them all and a second room had to be requisitioned, and then a third, and the lead investigator began to wonder, late at night when she could not sleep, whether the whole thing had been designed from the outset to consume exactly the kind of resources it was now consuming, as a kind of bureaucratic exhaustion attack, and whether the missing file had ever existed at all, or whether its absence was itself the message.
