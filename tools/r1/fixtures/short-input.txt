The library at the edge of the village had stood for seventy years before anyone noticed the door at the back of the reading room. It was painted the same dull green as the walls and fitted so flush that it disappeared when you stopped looking directly at it. The librarian, a woman named Sera, had worked there for eleven years and had never once tried the handle.

One Tuesday in October she stayed late to reshelve a delivery and, on her way to the supply closet, paused. The light from her single lamp fell across the door at an angle she had not seen before. She could make out a hairline gap along the jamb, and a faint draft from beneath it, smelling of dry stone and something else she could not place.

She did not open it that night. She wrote the date in her pocket notebook, locked the building, and went home. The next morning she came in two hours early and walked straight to the door without removing her coat. The handle turned without resistance. Beyond it was a narrow staircase descending into a dark she could not measure with her eyes alone.

She fetched the lamp.
